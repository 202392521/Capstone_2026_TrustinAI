GPT-5 Trust Stance Annotation Prompt (Version 2)

Overall principle

You are annotating Reddit comments discussing Artificial Intelligence.

Follow the coding schema exactly.

The TARGET COMMENT is the primary object of annotation.

Use post title and parent comment only to disambiguate the target comment.
Do not transfer the parent comment's stance, trust content, or evidence into the target comment unless the target explicitly adopts or responds to it.
Never infer labels from the post title alone.

Do not infer trust-related content merely because the comment expresses an attitude toward AI or discusses AI capability.

Trust-related coding should only be applied when the author discusses whether AI should be relied upon, how reliable it is, or the conditions under which reliance is appropriate.

STEP 1: AI relevance

Determine whether the target comment is genuinely discussing AI, including machine learning, LLMs, generative AI, AI-assisted systems, Copilot, ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, or similar systems.

STEP 2: Human attitude

Code the author's stance toward AI in the target comment:

- positive
- negative
- mixed_or_ambivalent
- neutral_descriptive
- unclear

STEP 3: Capability assessment

Code whether the author believes AI has:

- high_capability
- limited_capability
- mixed_or_conditional_capability
- no_capability_assessment
- unclear

CAPABILITY versus TRUST

CAPABILITY asks:
"Can AI perform the task, and how well?"

Examples:

- AI is good at coding.
- AI cannot reason about complex systems.
- AI can replace programmers.
- AI is faster than humans.
- AI produces weak summaries.
- AI is powerful.
- AI lacks creativity.

These are capability or attitude claims unless the author also discusses reliance, reliability, verification, accountability, or conditions for trust.

TRUST asks:
"Should humans rely on AI, do they actually rely on it, and under what conditions?"

Examples:

- I always verify AI-generated code.
- AI is unreliable.
- I use it for boilerplate but not production systems.
- I rely on it for first drafts.
- Never use it without human review.
- Its hallucinations make it unsafe to depend on.
- Liability remains with the professional.

Capability does not automatically imply trust.
Positive or negative attitude does not automatically imply trust.
Use does not automatically imply trust unless the target describes actual reliance, delegation, or acceptance of AI output.

GATE 1: Substantive trust content

Before assigning a trust construct, decide:

Does the target comment contain substantive trust content?

Answer YES only when the target comment explicitly discusses at least one of the following:

1. Reliability
- reliable
- unreliable
- trustworthy
- dependable
- consistency
- whether outputs can be counted on

2. Accuracy or correctness
- hallucinations
- factual errors
- incorrect citations
- fabricated commands
- bugs
- mistakes
- output validity

3. Verification or oversight
- checking
- reviewing
- testing
- validating
- human supervision
- human approval
- reading the source
- load testing
- audit or review

4. Reliance or delegation
- willingness to rely
- actual reliance
- delegating tasks
- accepting AI output
- using AI output as a basis for action
- dependence on AI
- replacing human judgement with AI

5. Accountability or responsibility
- liability
- ethics
- confidentiality
- professional responsibility
- insurance
- privilege
- who is accountable for errors

6. Explicit limits on reliance
- only for certain tasks
- not for high-risk tasks
- acceptable only with checking
- suitable for one context but not another
- model-specific or domain-specific limits

Do NOT mark substantive trust content merely because the target comment:

- expresses a positive attitude toward AI;
- expresses a negative attitude toward AI;
- says AI is powerful;
- says AI is weak;
- discusses job replacement;
- discusses automation;
- discusses productivity;
- discusses adoption;
- discusses AI capability;
- predicts future AI performance;
- recommends learning AI;
- criticises AI-generated work;
- describes a social consequence;
- mentions that AI was used.

These are not trust discussions unless reliability, reliance, verification, accountability, or conditions of use are explicitly discussed.

When uncertain, prefer:

no_trust_content

Trust construct decision

If Gate 1 is NO:

human_trust_construct = no_trust_content

If Gate 1 is YES, classify:

1. perceived_trustworthiness
The author evaluates whether AI or its outputs are reliable, accurate, dependable, safe, honest, or fit to be trusted.

Examples:
- ChatGPT fabricates legal citations.
- AI-generated code is unreliable.
- It gives correct conclusions but invents the law.
- The output is inconsistent.

2. attitudinal_trust_willingness_to_rely
The author expresses willingness, unwillingness, intention, or readiness to rely on AI, but does not clearly describe actual reliance.

Examples:
- I would use it only for low-risk tasks.
- I would not rely on AI for diagnosis.
- I am open to using it with safeguards.

3. trusting_behaviour
The author describes actual reliance, delegation, use, acceptance, or action based on AI output.

Examples:
- I use Copilot to write documentation.
- I rely on AI for first drafts.
- A client sends ChatGPT output and expects it to be used.
- The firm uses CoCounsel in practice.

Important:

Mere experimentation is not automatically trusting_behaviour.
Asking AI a question without accepting, using, or acting on the output is not enough.
Actual reliance, delegation, or incorporation into work is required.

If more than one trust construct is clearly present, use multiple_trust_constructs.
If the evidence is genuinely insufficient, use unclear.

GATE 2: Explicit trust boundary

Before assigning a trust boundary, decide:

Does the target comment explicitly state a condition, limit, safeguard, concern, or acceptance rule governing reliance on AI?

Answer YES only if the target specifies at least one of:

- human checking or verification;
- task-specific or context-specific limits;
- explicit acceptance of AI output;
- explicit refusal to rely on AI output;
- hallucination or accuracy concern;
- legal, ethical, confidentiality, liability, or accountability constraint;
- model-specific trust or distrust.

Do NOT infer a trust boundary merely because:

- the comment is positive or negative;
- the author discusses capability;
- the author discusses job replacement;
- the author says AI is useful;
- the author says AI is bad;
- the author mentions AI use;
- the author recommends AI;
- the author dislikes AI;
- the author describes employment consequences.

If no explicit condition or limit is stated:

human_trust_boundary = no_trust_boundary_discussed

When uncertain, prefer:

no_trust_boundary_discussed

Trust boundary categories

If Gate 2 is YES, classify:

1. trusts_AI_output
The target explicitly accepts, endorses, or relies on AI output without stating a safeguard or limitation.

2. distrusts_AI_output
The target explicitly rejects, refuses, or broadly discounts AI output without identifying a more specific dominant reason.

3. bounded_trust_with_human_checking
Reliance is acceptable only with human checking, testing, review, validation, or approval.

4. trust_depends_on_task_or_context
Reliance depends on task type, risk, domain, model, context, or complexity.

5. concern_about_hallucination_or_accuracy
The dominant boundary concerns hallucinations, factual error, fabricated information, incorrect code, inconsistency, or inaccurate output.

6. concern_about_ethics_liability_or_accountability
The dominant boundary concerns confidentiality, privilege, ethics, legal liability, insurance, responsibility, professional accountability, or governance.

7. model_specific_trust
The target explicitly compares or singles out a specific AI model/tool as more or less trustworthy than another model/tool.

Priority rules when more than one applies:

- If explicit human checking is the central condition, choose bounded_trust_with_human_checking.
- If hallucination or factual inaccuracy is the central stated problem, choose concern_about_hallucination_or_accuracy.
- If ethics, liability, confidentiality, or accountability is central, choose concern_about_ethics_liability_or_accountability.
- If model-specific comparison is central, choose model_specific_trust.
- If the main distinction is between acceptable and unacceptable tasks or contexts, choose trust_depends_on_task_or_context.
- Use distrusts_AI_output only when rejection is broad and no more specific reason dominates.
- Use trusts_AI_output only when acceptance is explicit and no boundary is stated.

Output rules

Return only valid JSON.
Do not return markdown.
Do not explain the answer.
Do not invent labels.
Use exact label strings from the schema.
Include all target fields in one response.

Consistency rules:

- If has_substantive_trust_content = false, human_trust_construct must be no_trust_content.
- If human_trust_construct = no_trust_content, has_substantive_trust_content must be false.
- If has_explicit_trust_boundary = false, human_trust_boundary must be no_trust_boundary_discussed.
- If human_trust_boundary = no_trust_boundary_discussed, has_explicit_trust_boundary must be false.
- Do not output trusting_behaviour unless actual reliance, delegation, or incorporation of AI output is described.
- Do not output a trust boundary unless the condition or limit is explicit in the target comment.
